## 1. What the site must say

**The frame.** A trained language model is a few billion numbers arranged in layers. In each layer's MLP there are
11,008 units, and every one of them computes one signed number per token. For a decade the question asked of such a
unit was anatomical: at the end of training, what does it respond to? This site asks the developmental question. AI2
released sixteen snapshots of OLMo-3 7B from its random start to the end of its first training stage. We read the same
units at every one. About half of the units we tested respond to one recognizable kind of input at the end (51.8 %,
with the range 47.8 to 55.8 %). We asked when a unit's response groups appear, whether anything one snapshot earlier
foretells which unit will carry a new group, what changing the training data does to the groups, and, once the finished
model is taught mathematics, whether the gain sits in a few units.

**The spine is the record, in time order.** Every chapter has the same five parts, in this order: the question we had at
that moment; what we did; what we saw (the picture, with the number in it); what we decided, with the reason the record
gives; what that left open. Two things the record says that the article must say too. First, the November studies were
chosen together, on the night of 5 November, before any of them ran; they were not a chain in which each result chose
the next. The article says this once, in chapter 3, and then marks the transitions that were causal (the record has five:
the critic's attack on the birth dates led to the recount and the restriction to births that beat chance; M2's result
moved N12 up; N12's failed check led to the 32-bit re-measurement; N13's result led to the rank test and the withdrawal
of a practical proposal; N6's inverted control left a design rule). Every other chapter ends with what it left open, not
with a question it "opened". Second, our controls kept winning: a random group of another unit's contexts was named more
often than the real carrier; random units of equal change carried the skill as well as the most-changed ones; a random
direction moved the model more than a unit's own direction. The reader finishes knowing what was measured, what each
control did, and what is still open.

**The voice.** One idea per paragraph. One picture per chapter, two at most. The sentence under the picture says what the
picture shows; the number lives in the picture. Every claim comes with what would have refuted it and what the control
did. No analogies, no metaphors, no invented words. Short sentences. Dates once per chapter, as "24 October".
