# Method note: high-background neurons in mean-replacement controls (REUSE SPEC4, 2026-11-02; revised after the different-model read)

**The rule's outcome first.** SPEC4 §1's H_hub said the high-background neurons are the whole generic term of every control arm. It was refuted on M2 as a point outcome: with those neurons excluded, the two-neuron null at the final checkpoint still exceeds the null at birth by 0.053 against the 0.05 line. The remainder's interval, from a joint bootstrap over pairs, is [0.025, 0.085] (seed 1; a second reader's seed gives [0.022, 0.077]), and 40 to 53 percent of draws fall under the line. So "not all" is supported; whether the remainder exceeds the line is close to even.

**The object.** In `allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B`, take each upstream MLP neuron's mean activation over 200 random corpus positions (ā, the value mean replacement writes). The top 1% by |ā| is 3,412 of 341,248 neurons over layers 0 to 30. At the final checkpoint the cut is 0.241 against a median of 0.003; at earlier checkpoints the cut is 17 to 56 times the median. They sit almost entirely in the last layers: layer 30 holds 1,575 and layers 0 to 18 hold 0 to 3 each. The published early-layer super-activation neurons (Yu et al. 2024; Oh et al. 2024) fire on a passage's first token and on delimiters. Either this model lacks them after its reordered norms, or ā over random non-first positions does not see them; the bank cannot say which. The nearest published object for the late-layer members is the high-norm final-layer neurons of Stolfo et al. 2024. No paper read before the freeze (`results/review4/LIT_high_background.md`, 45 opened) reports the effect of such neurons on attribution-ranked ablation sets or on mean-replacement controls.

**What they do to the controls.** An attribution-ranked driver set of 50 admits about three of them: 2,665 of the 43,800 own and cross members in the primary bank, 6.1 percent. They carry little of a region's direct attribution, a median 3 percent of the top-200 sum (90th percentile 12 percent). No arm held them alone; what was measured is every set re-ranked without them. Like for like (full-side sums, one weighting), the two-neuron null at the final checkpoint falls from 0.163 [0.110, 0.220] to 0.065 [0.034, 0.088], its 95th percentile from 0.468 to 0.376, and at birth from 0.031 to 0.012. The null's rise from birth to the final checkpoint falls from 0.132 to 0.053: 60 percent removed. A foreign neuron's drivers lowered the pole by 0.039 more than mean-matched random neurons at the final checkpoint; without the neurons the median difference is 0.011 [0.004, 0.033]. The real cases' ratio also falls, 0.375 to 0.294 at the final checkpoint and 0.226 to 0.207 at birth, because the old region's set carries them too. Per tercile the rule reads one null, neither, neither. In the lowest third the exclusion barely acts (the gap there was 0.043 and is 0.035); in the upper two it removes 70 and 60 percent of the gap, leaving 0.062 and 0.060.

**What it changes.** The final-checkpoint comparison already lay above its null before the exclusion (0.375 [0.303, 0.446] against 0.163, no overlap). With the exclusion it is a pre-registered rule with a room gate (0.296 over the 0.200 line) and a null 0.10 lower: 0.294 against 0.065. The first pre-registration's rule R1 compared the birth median with the final-checkpoint null and was refuted by an overlap of intervals; redone with the exclusion, that comparison does not overlap (0.207 [0.156, 0.291] against 0.065 [0.034, 0.088]). That is an exploratory reading, since §6 attached "R1's refutation is explained" to H_hub holding, and H_hub was refuted. The rise of the ratio over training is not distinguishable from the rise between two halves of one cluster (difference −0.004 [−0.039, 0.030]).

**Mechanism, as an inference.** This model normalises each MLP's output before adding it to the residual stream. Replacing one large-|ā| neuron's output by its mean changes that norm's scale at the position. That rescales every other neuron of the layer, so a neuron that carries little direct attribution can move the pole through the scale. Stolfo et al. 2024 §3.2 separated such a path at the final norm by holding the scale fixed during the replacement. The same control at every upstream layer is the next instrument (`NEXT.md`, queue item 2); it was not run here.

**Practice.** A mean-replacement control on attribution-ranked sets in this model should exclude the top 1 percent by background activation from every set, as SPEC4 did, or match on them. It should report the null's 95th percentile with and without them, under one construction on both sides. Sources: `results/birth/verdict_birth.json`, `results/birth/recount_fable.txt`, `results/birth/before_nulls.json`, `results/review4/hub_split.out`, `results/REVIEW_birth.md`.

**Addendum, 2026-11-02 20:15 CST, after the sanity check (`results/sanity/SANITY_science.md` item 4).** "The top 1% by |ā|, almost entirely in the last layers" describes the set's bulk, and that bulk acts on almost nothing: a layer's median |ā| rises about 100-fold from layer 0 to layer 30 (at T_15 from 0.00033 to 0.042), so the global cut takes 7.7 to 14.3% of layer 30 and almost nothing below layer 20, and a pole's upstream sets never admit the last layers. The members that enter sets and move the controls are 10 to 131 distinct neurons per load, mostly in layers 8 to 25, at a median 129 to 639 times their own layer's median: within-layer outliers, closer to the published super-activation objects than the paragraph above allows. The two-neuron null's pairs sit in layers 5, 15 and 26 only while the cases span layers 4 to 31, so the null and the cases see different amounts of the set. The practice paragraph stands with one change: the exclusion should be defined per layer (a within-layer outlier rule), not by a global cut.

**Addendum 2, 2026-11-02 23:20 CST, revised 23:50 CST after the Opus read (SPEC6: `SPEC6_generic.md`, `results/REVIEW_generic.md`, Results 38 to 43).** The mechanism paragraph above ("replacing one large-|ā| neuron's output by its mean changes that norm's scale at the position, which rescales every other neuron of the layer") is withdrawn as the account of the term. Holding the post-feedforward norm's divisor at its clean value during the replacement does not lower the controls: like for like on the pairs where the held forward is finite, the unexcluded two-neuron null goes from 0.130 to 0.188 and the 1%-excluded one from 0.040 to 0.068, the members-alone fall from 0.086 to 0.139, and the gap between the unexcluded and excluded nulls survives holding (0.092 free, 0.074 held on the 12 pairs finite in every condition). So the term does not run through the ablated layers' norm scale. Whether it is a direct contribution is not established: the held excess appears in pairs with no high-background member (layer 5, 0.039 to 0.115) and tracks the held instrument's own runaway (it compounds across a run of held layers after a within-layer outlier and is non-finite in 209 of 1,730 held files). The held-scale instrument at upstream layers is not usable as defined. The practice paragraph stands; a per-layer 1% cut lowers the free null further (0.015 against 0.067) but also lowers own falls and removes far more neurons, so it is not yet the exclusion's definition.

**Addendum 3, 2026-11-03 04:45 CST (10:45Z), after the ALWAYSON side tests (`../alwayson/SPEC_qwen_alwayson.md`, `../alwayson/SPEC2_edit.md`).** On Qwen3.5-0.8B the practice question "are the high-background neurons a target list for behaviour edits?" was put to the released loop edit three ways. The editor's 128 loop-writing neurons are mostly outside their block's top 1 percent by |ā| (117 of 128; SPEC1, H_on refuted). The 64 largest-|ā| columns per edited block carry 1.3 and 2.0 percent of the loop direction's writing capacity Σ(u·W)², the random level (95th percentiles 3.3 and 3.8; `../alwayson/results/edit/offline_fable.json`). Projecting the loop direction out of those columns lowers the repetition share in generation by 0.09 [0.00, 0.18] against the editor's 0.31 [0.22, 0.39] and a norm-matched random set's 0.05 [−0.04, 0.14] (SPEC2; exploratory, the frozen flag having saturated at the 4,096-token cap). None of the three separates the set from a random set. In this model the set is a property of mean-replacement controls, not a lever for this edit.

**Addendum 4, 2026-11-03 18:45 CST (00:45Z), after INSTRUMENTS E1 and E1b and their independent reads (`studies/instruments/SPEC_instruments.md`, "After freeze 1a", "After freeze 1c" and their errata; `studies/instruments/results/RESULTS.md` Results 4 to 10 and 18 to 19).** The practice paragraph above is revised in three ways. (1) *The written value matters, and zero is the cleanest choice on this model when paired with the exclusion.* On SPEC4's 56 planted pairs at T_15 the two-neuron null with the global top-1 percent exclusion is 0.147 under resample replacement, 0.067 under the mean, 0.049 under the median and 0.007 [−0.002, 0.032] under zero (95th percentiles 0.338, 0.384, 0.434, 0.210), with the own falls unchanged across values; the real cases' reuse ratio is 0.293 under zero against 0.294 under the mean, and 91 of 150 cases clear their rule's line under zero with the exclusion against 57 of 149 under the mean. Zero without the exclusion gives the highest line (0.575). Resample ablation raises every null and the real ratio alike (0.362) and is the worst choice on this readout. (2) *The members' push is a real dependence, not a first-order marked-token effect.* Resampling leaves the members-alone fall unchanged (1.06 [0.78, 1.21]), zero halves it, and the direct term Σ (a_k − v_k) c_k at the marked token predicts a median 0.0004 against a measured 0.066 on that arm; the members in the pairs' sets are 34 neurons in layers 7 to 25 at a median 145 times their layer's median |ā|, all dense; the exclusion stays the handling and the push's path is not named. (3) *The term is Olmo-3's.* On gemma-3-4b-pt and Qwen3-4B-Base at their final weights, with the same construction through a model adapter whose Olmo path is bitwise E1's, the median two-neuron null is 0.004 and 0.003 under the mean (cross falls 0.002 in absolute terms against Olmo's 0.074, own falls 0.66 and 0.53) and stays near zero under every value; gemma's outlier members act only in the tail (the exclusion lowers its 95th percentile from 0.235 to 0.060) and under zero (an unexcluded line of 0.711). The post-feedforward norm is not sufficient for the term (Gemma 3 has one, Qwen 3 none); the pre-MLP norm that both have and Olmo lacks and the members' scale (145 against 29 and 17 times the layer median) are the open differences (a code audit found no bug; the cross push on gemma is present but bidirectional, and the term is indirect on every model: 47 times its first-order value on Olmo); E1b's construction never ran on Olmo, and a 0.3 GPU-h relaunch separates model from construction, and a within-layer exclusion on Olmo is untested. The method note's rule for a new model: measure the two-neuron null under the mean with and without the exclusion first; where it is near zero, as on these two, no exclusion or value change is needed.

**Addendum 5, 2026-11-04 15:45 CST (21:45Z), after INSTRUMENTS E5, E6a and E6c and their independent reads (`studies/instruments/SPEC_instruments.md`, "After freeze 5", "After freeze 6" and their errata; `results/RESULTS.md` Results 28 to 33).** Three things change in the practice for Olmo-3. (1) *The construction is not the difference between models.* E1b's construction run on Olmo-3 gives a two-neuron null of 0.128 under the mean (gemma and Qwen 0.004 and 0.003 under the same code), so the term is the model's. (2) *The term's channel is the other positions.* Writing the replacement at the marked token alone leaves the pairs' null at 0.005 [−0.001, 0.028] (95th percentile 0.098 excluded; 0.146 unexcluded); holding the attention inputs' pre-norm scale at the target layer removes only a fifth of the term (0.043 against 0.054, not separated), so the push is not mainly the target layer's norm. Whether Olmo's always-on members write more of the residual's length than gemma's is not settled: every registered statistic has no power on a planted doubling (E6a cancelled). (3) *The rule for Olmo-3 names two acceptable instruments.* Zero at every position with the global exclusion (null median 0.007, 95th percentile 0.205 [0.103, 0.376]; real cases 0.293) and the mean written at the marked token only with the exclusion (null median 0.004, 95th percentile 0.098 [0.047, 0.206]; real cases 0.231 [0.187, 0.286], the random arm 0.002 of the own fall) are both acceptable; neither is shown cleaner than the other; the mean at every position (0.067 / 0.384) is not acceptable. The marked-token write removes the same absolute amount from real cases and from unrelated pairs (0.019 against 0.021), so the two instruments differ only in the reuse effect both contain; the median real case keeps 0.89 of its free ratio and 19 of 149 lose more than 0.15, so a per-case reading should report both. The rule for a new model from Addendum 4 stands: measure the two-neuron null under the mean with and without the exclusion first. Whether the term exists at OLMo-2's final weights (E6b) is pending.
