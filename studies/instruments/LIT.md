## E1: the written value; outliers in controls; first-order prediction

- **Li & Janson 2024, Optimal ablation for interpretability** (NeurIPS; arXiv 2409.09951; read §2.1–2.4, App. D–E.3). On GPT-2-small heads and MLP blocks (IOI): "Δ_(opt) accounts for only 11.1% of Δ_(zero), 33.0% of Δ_(mean), and 17.7% of Δ_(resample) for the median component" (§2.4, Table 1); mean single-component loss gap 0.0584 zero, 0.0405 mean, 0.0559 resample (App. E.3, Table 2); the excess over the optimal constant "is entirely caused by spoofing" (§2.3). Different object (whole components, loss) but **against** H_value's direction: resampling writes another input's information and read higher than the mean. Changes: a §7 threat (resample can raise own and cross falls); bank and report the per-draw falls and the median over the 4 draws beside their mean.
- **Pochinkov, Pasero, Shibayama 2024, Peak Activation Centering** (2408.17322; read §3–§5). Pruning attention neurons in OPT-1.3b and Mistral-7b: "Peak ablation has the most consistent pattern, causing the lowest amount of degradation, with mean ablation and zero ablation coming a close second and third, and Random resampling causes by far the most degradation" (§4.1; their resample uses random-token inputs). **Consistent with** the median arm as the floor; **against** resample being the low arm. Changes: read D1m (median) beside D1 in R1; the median arm is the cleaner test of H_value.
- **Miller, Chughtai, Saunders 2024, Circuit faithfulness metrics are not robust** (2407.08734; read §3–§6, App.). "using Resample Ablations from the ABC distribution ... results in a systematically lower faithfulness as compared with mean ablations" (§4.1); "The task cannot be separated from the ablation methodology." (§6). **Consistent with** E1's premise. Changes: A1 states each false-positive line as a property of the rule and the set construction.
- **McGrath et al. 2023, The Hydra effect** (2307.15771; read §2–§3, App. A). Resample "is probably the most principled, as every ablation is a naturally-occurring set of activations, and so is more likely to respect properties of network activations such as emergent outliers" (App. A); resample ablations "require a reasonably large sample size to prevent excessive noise" (§2.5.2). **Predicted by** (as an argument, not a measurement) H_value. Changes: R = 4 draws may be noisy; report the draw-to-draw SD per pair beside the common-sense check.
- **Zhang & Nanda 2024** (2309.16042; read §1–§3, §6) and **Heimersheim & Nanda 2024** (2404.15255; read §1–§2.5). Off-distribution concern "presents a challenge to any intervention techniques that introduce OOD inputs to the model or its internal layers, including ablations" (Zhang §6); mean ablation "is slightly more principled than zero ablating since there is no special meaning to activations being zero" (Heimersheim §2.5). **Consistent with**; neither compares values on neuron sets. Changes: nothing.
- **Han, Andreas, Fedorenko, de Varda 2026, Modular cognitive architecture** (2608.13567; PDF read §1.5 Methods, SI §5–6). Nearest design: top-0.1% attribution-ranked neurons of one task ablated on another task under counterfactual, mean and zero values (Qwen2.5-32B): "All three methods produce a consistent modular pattern" and "Counterfactual ablation produces the largest accuracy drop" (SI §6). No random-set control, no outlier analysis. **Consistent with** a value-robust cross contrast; does not test the generic term. Changes: cite as the nearest published cross-set ablation under several values.
- **Sun, Canziani, LeCun, Zhu 2026, Anatomy of massive activations and attention sinks** (2603.05498; re-read §4.2.2). "Sandwich normalization reduces spikes while preserving a sink ratio nearly identical to the baseline"; "Replacing the block-level norm with QKNorm almost entirely eliminates spikes" (§4.2.2, Table 5). Olmo 3, Gemma 3 and Qwen 3 all use QK-norm. Changes: E1b's contrast (post-feedforward norm or none) does not separate residual-spike regimes; the high-background members are within-layer MLP outliers, so H_model should not cite the massive-activation literature as the mechanism.
- **Kramár, Lieberum, Shah, Nanda 2024, AtP*** (2403.00745; read §2–§3, §5). "smaller components (e.g. MLP neurons or attention heads) are more numerous but more linear, likely yielding better results from gradient-based methods like AtP" (§5.4); false negatives from saturation and from direct/indirect cancellation (§3.1); set estimators assume "approximate node additivity" (§3.3). **Predicted by** for H_linear on single neurons. Changes: report r and slope on the members-alone arm and the own arm separately (R4 pools them); the 50-member sets test additivity.
- **Zhang & Wang 2026, When attribution patching lies** (2606.09899; read §1–§4, App. E.2). First-order relative error 4.3% for post-activation neurons against 35.2% pre-activation (Pythia-410M, App. E.2, Table 11); "gains on post-activation neurons are small (2–7%), consistent with the near-linearity of activations after the nonlinearity has already been applied" (§4.2). **Predicted by** H_linear for single-neuron writes at the post-activation, the site E1 writes. Changes: none; the set-level part is the open one.
- **Rezaei Jafari, Eberle, Khakzar, Nanda 2025, RelP** (2508.21258; read §1–§5, App. B). First-order (AtP) against activation patching on whole MLP outputs: Pearson 0.006 (GPT-2 large), 0.471 (Qwen2-7B), 0.910 (Gemma2-2B) (App. B, Table 5). **Against** a model-independent r. Changes: keep R4 reported, not a rule; E1's Δ_lin uses the direct coefficient c_k only, so its slope is the direct share, as R4 says.

## E2: column-level projection edits for repetition

- **The editor's record** (Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, Khattab 2026; read `sources/exp8_math_README.md` and, from github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs, `exp8_math/runs/p50c/tool_calls.jsonl` line 62 and `work_notes/final_report.md`). Rule: score = (u·W[:, n]) × (mean activation over the last 24 tokens, repetitive minus productive) on 4 matched pairs (fwd_4), top 64 by argsort. Against 6 other pairs (fwd_24) the log prints "transfer rank correlation 0.666 top64 overlap 55" (block 15) and "transfer rank correlation 0.583 top64 overlap 36" (block 19), and "positive mass top8/32/64 [0.193, 0.389, 0.498]" and "[0.397, 0.498, 0.555]". Final: "W = W0 - 6*outer(u, mask64*(u@W0))"; "Norm-matched orthogonal random control gained1.56pp SE3.52" (by its name a random direction orthogonal to u, not random columns); at max 32,768 tokens "median_correct_tokens": 9171.5 edited against 5310.5 original. Changes: (a) the editor's own context-to-context recovery is 91 of 128, the reference for P64 ∩ E (prediction 70 [40, 110]); (b) E's correct answers have a median length above the 8,192 cap, so E's accuracy and cut flags at 8,192 understate its effect; keep the rep flag primary; (c) the README's "64 output columns ... that write it most" is not the rule; cite the code; (d) the program's "91% and 98%" share (`studies/alwayson/sources/NOTE_2026-11-02_weightspace.md` §1) is not the printed 0.498 and 0.555; reconcile before quoting.
- **Lazaridis, Sharma, Bates, King, Lu, FitzGerald 2026, When can one neuron fix repetition loops in LLMs?** (2606.13705; read §1–§7). Closest published work. Down_proj columns ranked by gradient×activation on loop positions; "Within the same prompt, neuron-rank Spearman correlation and top-100 overlap average 0.056/0.097 for E4B and 0.122/0.205 for 31B" (§4); random controls use "the selected edit’s layer, operation, and unit count while excluding the selected units" (§4: count-matched); on Qwen3.5-4B "A 100-neuron L12 sign-inversion edit reduces positives from 181 to 104" (§6.2); a repetition penalty of 1.30 "breaks code generation" (§3). **Against** stable single-rollout rankings; no comparison of ranking factors, so E2 is not done. Changes: add a split-half stability check of P (rank correlation and top-64 overlap between two halves of the 200 base generations); note that s = 6 sets each column's u-component to −5 times its value, the sign-inversion family (their α < 0).
- **Hiraoka & Inui 2025, Repetition neurons** (2410.13497; read §2–§5, App.). Ranking A is theirs: mean activation 30 tokens after onset minus 30 before (§3.1). Detector: "the same 10-gram token sequence appeared three times at equal intervals within 100 tokens"; "The onset of repetition is defined as the point where the repeated sequence appears for the second time" (§2.2). Zeroing the top neurons "reduces the number of samples with repetition by up to 25% (and by as much as 35% with optimal hyperparameter settings" (§4.1). **Predicted by**: R_A > 0 and below R_E. Changes: the spec puts onset at the first occurrence; the source at the second; state the choice and keep the planted-onset gate (100) consistent with it.
- **Yang et al. 2025, How does DPO reduce toxicity?** (2411.06424; read §1–§5). Neurons chosen by direction alone (top 256 value vectors by cosine to the probe) "account for as little as 2.5% to 24% of the reduction in toxicity scores" (§4); per-neuron contribution is activation × (value · probe), "direct feature attribution" (§3). **Consistent with** H_direction refuted (R_D < R_E). Changes: none.
- **Arditi et al. 2024, Refusal is mediated by a single direction** (2406.11717; read §4.1, App. E, G.3). "W′_out ← W_out − r̂r̂ᵀW_out" on every matrix that writes the residual stream, equivalent to directional ablation (§4.1); collateral read as CE loss on the Pile and on the model's own completions (App. G.3). Changes: name E as column-restricted weight orthogonalization at strength 6; E2's Δloss and KL readouts match published practice.
- **Sun et al. 2026, Steer2Edit** (2602.09870; read §3–§4, App. E). Components ranked by g_i = cos(v_i, W_i μ_i) ("Importance weighting", §3); the raw dot product instead drops the combined score from 0.427 to 0.039 (App. E.1, Table 3); shorter reasoning on Qwen3-4B-Thinking at matched accuracy (§4). **Against** unnormalized products across layers; E2 ranks within one block, but P is a raw product. Changes: a threat line (P may be led by a few large-contrast neurons); report P's top-64 overlap with A.
- **Ferrara 2026, Open-weight masked introspection** (2608.20569; read §1–§3.4). "Norm matching alone systematically understates the control. A random direction in a high-dimensional residual space lands nearly orthogonal to the feature manifold, so it does less damage at the same norm" (§3.4); its control is calibrated to equal output divergence. **Predicted by** H_match, as a design argument, not a measured comparison. Changes: cite as the precedent for R_KL.
- **Qwen3.5-0.8B model card** (huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B README, read 2026-11-03), **vLLM SamplingParams** docstring (`vllm/sampling_params.py`), **Pipis et al. 2025** (2512.12895; read §1–§2). ## E3: partitions of a neuron's contexts by its inputs

- **Dreyer, Purelku, Vielhaben, Samek, Lapuschkin 2024, PURE** (2404.06453; read in full). For a neuron's top-n_ref samples, the lower-layer Gradient×Activation attribution vectors are clustered by k-means into "virtual neurons" (§3); "PURE leads to more disentangled representations than activation-based clustering, and is performance-wise close to DINOv2" and "PURE has higher alignment to CLIP compared to activations" (§4.2; ResNets on ImageNet, k = 2 to 5). E3's V is this method in a language model, with the clusterer in CLIP's place. **Consistent with** H_input. Changes: cite PURE as the method's origin; the novelty is the language-model test with a token control, a permutation null and a self-agreement ceiling.
- **Golimblevskaia et al. 2025, CircuitLens** (2510.14936; read §1–§5, App. E.4–E.5). Inputs of a Gemma-2-2B transcoder feature clustered by their contributing upstream features and heads (Jaccard, DBSCAN; §3.3); "Layer 0 shows almost no underlying circuit structure beyond its own attention heads, producing mainly token-based activations ... averaging only 1.05 clusters per feature"; layers 7, 12, 21 average 4.5, 2.8, 2.95 (§5). **Against** §5.5's detector prediction (0.6 flagged in layers 0–3, 0.4 deeper); **predicted by** for H_token in low layers. Changes: report R1, R2 and the detector by layer band; revisit the depth prediction before the freeze.
- **Shabalin, Piotrowski et al. 2026, Many transcoder features are neurons in disguise** (2610.24521; read §2.5, App. R.4–R.5). Cosine K-means (K = 16) on dense MLP activations gives clusters a Claude Sonnet judge separates at 0.933 ± 0.006 (App. R.5, Table 15), with "the eight globally densest neurons ... masked out so cluster weights are more interpretable" (App. R.5). **Consistent with** H_input (input-space clusters are readable). Changes: mask E1's high-background members out of v_x, or report both, since a few large members can dominate a cosine distance; this is also the only published exclusion of dense neurons found (in clustering, not ablation).
- **Cammarata 2026, Neural networks are already clean** (Goodfire essay, already-clean.goodfire.com, text read 2026-11-03). "we also cluster the attribution flowing into the neuron", giving "bundles of upstream neurons" (section "It beats anything we found"); in Qwen3-32B "roughly 90% of neuron poles have one main job ... another ~7% have two related jobs" ("The Short Version"); of the double-negative SwiGLU "echo": "for about 4% of neurons, it’s the whole top of the list" ("Neuron paper"). Same vocabulary as the program (poles, jobs, echo). **Against** the multi-job shares of §5.5 as a prior. Changes: a signed driver vector separates echo contexts by sign and can inflate ARI(V, C) through the clusterer's echo clusters; report R1 with echo clusters removed.
- **Kopf et al. 2025, PRISM** (2506.15538; read §3–§4). Sentence-embedding k-means (k = 5) on high-percentile contexts with one LLM label per cluster (§3); "polysemanticity scores show no consistent trend across layers" (§4.4). The published analogue of the W4 clusterer. Changes: none.
- **Cho, Roh, Kim 2026, Shared semantics, divergent mechanisms** (2606.08236; read §1–§4). Clustering continuations by a semantic embedding or by an attribution signature: "KM-S preserves semantic coherence but incurs high mechanistic distortion, whereas KM-A reduces mechanistic distortion at the cost of semantic coherence" (§4). **Consistent with** a low ARI(V, C). Changes: none.
- **Bricken et al. 2023, Towards monosemanticity** ("Feature Splitting"). "We see more of these features as we increase the total number of learned sparse features, a phenomenon we refer to as feature splitting"; "determining the 'correct number of features' for dictionary learning is less important than it might initially seem". Changes: V and C may cut one job at different granularities; the silhouette-k ARI the spec already reports is the check; read k_V = k_C agreement as a lower bound.
- **Romano, Vinh, Bailey, Verspoor 2016** (JMLR; arXiv 1512.01286; read §1–§6) and **Sundqvist, Chiquet, Rigaill 2022** (2011.08708; read abstract, §1–§3). "ARI might be used as external validation index when the reference clustering shows big equal sized clusters. AMI can be used when the reference clustering is unbalanced and there exist small clusters" (Romano §6); choosing among candidate partitions favours more clusters (§5.2); ARI's adjustment "forces the size of the clusters" and the ARI–MARI gap "can be large for small n" (Sundqvist, abstract). Changes: report AMI beside ARI (n = 50, unbalanced reference partitions); the per-pole permutation null with fixed sizes is the right chance line; the silhouette k carries selection bias.
- **Nagpal 2025, Generation length under a fixed context window** (Meta; chiragnagpal.github.io PDF, read §1–§2). Truncated generations are right-censored; "estimators that account for right-censoring result in more accurate and unbiased estimate" (abstract). **Consistent with** the spec's Kaplan–Meier onset; no paper found applies it to repetition onset. Changes: none.

## Summary

1. E1 is not published: no paper among the 45 earlier and the 37 opened here reports within-layer outlier neurons driving ablation controls or nulls, or compares mean, zero, median and resample on attribution-ranked sets against a two-neuron null.
2. E1's main threat: two measurements read resample as more damaging than mean (Li & Janson 2024, loss gap 0.0559 against 0.0405; Pochinkov et al. 2024). The spoofing account lets resample raise falls, against H_value's direction; the median arm is the cleaner test.
3. E1 first order: single post-activation neurons are near-linear (4.3% error, Zhang & Wang 2026); whole-MLP first-order r spans 0.006 to 0.910 by model (RelP). Additivity for sets of about 50 neurons written at every window position was not found measured.
4. E2's nearest work, Lazaridis et al. 2026, edits down_proj columns to cut loops in Gemma 4 and Qwen3.5-4B. Its single-rollout rankings are unstable (Spearman 0.06 to 0.12), its random controls are count-matched, and it compares no ranking factors, so E2 is not done.
5. E2's own source: the editor's two context sets share 91 of 128 top-64 columns (Spearman 0.67, 0.58). At the 32,768 cap the edited model's correct answers have a median of 9,172 tokens, above E2's 8,192 cap.
6. E2 controls: selection by direction alone explained 2.5 to 24% in the DPO case (Yang et al. 2025), and divergence matching is argued to beat norm matching (Ferrara 2026). Both point the way the spec predicts; neither measures this edit.
7. E3's method is published: PURE (2024) in vision, where attribution-vector k-means beats activation clustering against CLIP; CircuitLens (2025) for transcoder features; Goodfire (2026) informally for Qwen3 neurons. A quantitative test against an LLM text clusterer, with a token control and a permutation null, was not found.
8. E3's main threat: CircuitLens finds about 1 circuit cluster per feature at layer 0 and 2.8 to 4.5 deeper, the opposite of §5.5's depth prediction for the detector. Goodfire's 90% one-job poles sits far below the predicted multi-job shares.
9. Lines for the spec: mask high-background members from v_x; report AMI with ARI; report E3 R1 without echo clusters; add a split-half stability check of P; state E2's onset definition and decoding temperature against their sources.
10. None of E1, E2 or E3 is already published as specified. E3's method is not new (PURE); its test is.
